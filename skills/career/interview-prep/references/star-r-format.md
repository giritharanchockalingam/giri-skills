# STAR+R Format (Situation, Task, Action, Result + Reflection)

## Structure and timing (≈90 seconds spoken, ≤120 words written)
| Part | Share | What to say | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Situation | 15% | Company context, scale, why it mattered, one sentence | Backstory, org charts |
| Task | 10% | Your mandate and constraint ("I was asked to… within…") | Team's task without your role |
| Action | 45% | 3 decisions **you** made, in order, with the trade-off each carried | "We" without "I"; activity lists |
| Result | 20% | Number from the source, business consequence, timeline | Vague "it went well" |
| Reflection | 10% | What you would do differently or what you now apply by default | Humble-bragging, blaming others |

## Template
```
[SB-nn] <Competency code> — <Story title>
S: At <company> (<scale>), <situation>.
T: I was accountable for <mandate> with <constraint>.
A: First, I <decision 1> because <trade-off>. Second, I <decision 2>. Third, I <decision 3>.
R: <Metric from source> in <timeframe>; <business consequence>.
R+: Looking back, <lesson>; I now <changed behavior>.
Source: [SB-nn] / [MR-Lnn]
```

## Scoring rubric (mock mode; 1–5 each, 25 max)
| Criterion | 5 | 3 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | All five parts, in order, ≤90s | Parts present, over time | Rambling, no result |
| Specificity | Named decisions and trade-offs | Generic actions | Buzzwords |
| Scale | Matches target level (people, $, stakeholders) | One level below | Individual contributor |
| Result | Quantified, sourced, business-framed | Qualitative | Missing |
| Reflection | Concrete behavior change | Platitude | Missing |

Feedback format: score line, one strength, one fix, one re-ask prompt.

## Reframing rules (truth-locked)
- The same story may serve two competencies; change the emphasis in Action, not the facts.
- Emphasis rotation: C2 leads with the money decision; C3 leads with the people decision; C6 leads with the conversation that changed a mind.
- A Reflection sentence proposed by the skill is marked `[confirm]` until the user agrees it is true.

## Failure stories
Every exec interview asks for one. Pick a real one from the bank where: the stake was large, the decision was yours, the recovery is measurable, and the lesson is still in use. Do not substitute a disguised success.
