## Article 4 as it stands (S2, S3) — brief EU-law quotation
1. "Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf, taking into account their technical knowledge, experience, education and training and the context the AI systems are to be used in, and considering the persons or groups of persons on whom the AI systems are to be used. This obligation does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual."
2. The Commission and Member States support providers and deployers (in particular SMEs) and the Commission publishes practical examples on the single information platform (Art. 62(3)(b)).
3. The AI Board adopts recommendations on AI literacy, taking into account European competence frameworks.

## What changed
The 2024 text required measures "to ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy". The Digital Omnibus on AI replaced this with measures "to support the development of" AI literacy and added that no specific individual level must be guaranteed (S3, S7). The obligation still applies to providers and deployers. Briefs that cite the "sufficient level" wording are out of date.

## How the skill uses this
- The plan is a record of measures taken; the Commission says no certificate is needed (S7).
- Role-based modules reflect "technical knowledge, experience, education and training and the context" (S2).
- The overseer rule (human-oversight module always required) reflects Art. 26(2) for high-risk deployers (S6); the skill applies it to every overseer as a design choice.
- Not a legal determination. Whether the organisation is a provider or deployer, and whether a system is high-risk, is for counsel.
