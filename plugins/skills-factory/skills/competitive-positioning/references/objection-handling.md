# Objection Handling

## 1. Response structure
| Step | Purpose | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledge | Show the objection is legitimate | One sentence; no "but" |
| Reframe | Move from their framing to the consequence that matters | Tie to a client-stated priority |
| Proof | Evidence we handle it | Cleared reference with a number, or a specific mechanism (named team, harness, clause) |
| Ask-back | Return control; learn their real concern | A question that surfaces the decision criterion behind the objection |

Never argue with the alternative's strength. Argue with its consequence.

## 2. Objections by alternative class
### Incumbent services provider
| Objection | Acknowledge | Reframe | Proof pattern | Ask-back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "They know our systems; you would need months to ramp." | True that context matters. | Context built over years also carries assumptions nobody has re-examined; the question is what an independent look finds. | Assessment-phase mechanism: inventory and scoring in <n> weeks; reference where baseline found <x>. | "When was the current run-rate last baselined independently?" |
| "Switching is risky mid-program." | Risk is real. | Risk concentrates at cutover, not at partner change; a parallel-run harness governs that risk regardless of who runs it. | Reference with parallel-run outcome. | "What is the rollback approach in the current plan?" |
| "They are cheaper because they are already here." | Lower onboarding cost is real. | Compare total run-rate over 3 years, not onboarding; price from a scored inventory is comparable, a legacy rate is not. | TCO structure from `integration-migration-assessor`. | "Could we see the current cost per interface per year?" |

### Platform vendor services arm
| Objection | Acknowledge | Reframe | Proof pattern | Ask-back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Nobody knows the product better than the vendor." | Product depth is real. | Your estate is multi-platform; the outcome depends on the seams between products, and an independent team owns the seams. | Cross-platform reference. | "Which of your integrations touch only one vendor's product?" |
| "One throat to choke." | Accountability matters. | Accountability for the outcome is contractual; a vendor's services arm is accountable to the vendor's licence targets first. | SOW acceptance criteria and SLA structure. | "How is the services team's success measured — your outcome or product adoption?" |
| "They bundle services with the licence discount." | Bundles can be attractive. | A discount tied to services locks the services price; separate the decisions and compare on like-for-like scope. | Commercial transparency: rate card, scored estimate. | "Does the discount survive if services go elsewhere?" |

### Global SI
| Objection | Acknowledge | Reframe | Proof pattern | Ask-back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "They have done this 100 times." | Scale is real. | The team that did it 100 times is rarely the team that shows up; ask for named leads and continuity commitments. | Named team, continuity clause, leverage model. | "Will the proposed leads be contractually committed for the program?" |
| "Procurement is more comfortable with a big name." | Comfort matters. | Comfort should come from the contract: fixed price from a scored inventory, acceptance criteria, service credits. | SOW structures. | "What in the contract gives you the comfort, beyond the logo?" |

### In-house build
| Objection | Acknowledge | Reframe | Proof pattern | Ask-back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "We can do it ourselves cheaper." | No margin is real. | The cost is opportunity and time: what does the team not do while building this, and what does delay cost per quarter? | Cost-of-delay from value case; BOT model. | "What is the current team's capacity against the roadmap?" |
| "We will lose knowledge if a partner builds it." | Knowledge retention matters. | Build-operate-transfer with paired teams and documentation gates retains it; a stretched in-house build often leaves no documentation at all. | KT deliverables with acceptance criteria. | "What does knowledge transfer look like in the current plan?" |

### Status quo
| Objection | Acknowledge | Reframe | Proof pattern | Ask-back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "It works well enough." | It does run. | The forcing event (contract end, EOL, peak) arrives on its own schedule; the question is whether you meet it with a plan or a war room. | Discovery quotes on incidents; dates. | "What happens on <forcing date> if nothing changes?" |
| "No budget this year." | Budget cycles are real. | A short fixed-fee assessment fits discretionary budget and produces the business case for next cycle. | Assessment offer. | "What would the business case need to show to get funded?" |

## 3. Objections about us (prepare honestly)
| Objection | Likely truth | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "You are smaller than the alternatives." | Often true | Named team, senior allocation, commercial commitments, escalation to leadership |
| "You have not worked with us before." | True | Assessment phase as a low-risk first step; references in the vertical |
| "Offshore delivery worries our security team." | Legitimate | Data-residency design, access model, certifications in scope, onshore-only options with price impact stated |
| "Your price is higher." | Possibly | Show basis: scored inventory, P80 contingency explicit; invite like-for-like comparison; levers that change price with stated risk effects |

## 4. Rules
- Proof only from cleared references; no "we did this for <uncleared client>".
- No disparagement of the alternative by name; consequence language only.
- Every response ends with a question.
- Rehearse in `orals-prep`; this file is the source.
