# Competitive Method

## 1. Alternative classes (hypothesis patterns)
Use as starting hypotheses. Confirm or kill each with evidence before it influences the proposal.

| Class | Typical strengths | Typical weaknesses | Their selling motion | Where we usually win |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Incumbent services provider | Relationship, context, no onboarding friction, knows the landmines | Current run-rate and assumptions are theirs; innovation fatigue; staff rotation; priced-in complacency | "Continuity, lower risk, we know your systems" | Independent baseline and audit of current cost; fresh pattern (factory, automation); fixed-price confidence from scoring |
| Platform vendor services arm | Product depth, roadmap access, implicit warranty, bundled pricing | Optimizes for platform adoption not client outcome; weak on cross-platform integration; expensive senior rates; services as licence lever | "Nobody knows the product better; one throat to choke" | Multi-platform estates; migration away from or between platforms; independence; outcome ownership |
| Global SI | Scale, references, bench, methodology, procurement comfort | Bench staffing swaps; layered management; slow to mobilize; high overhead; generic approach | "Scale and safety; we have done this 100 times" | Named-team continuity; speed to mobilize; price via leverage; vertical specificity |
| Boutique / specialist | Deep niche skill, senior hands-on, fast | Scale limits, bus factor, weaker on governance, thin balance sheet for large fixed price | "Experts, not juniors" | Scale with governance; offshore leverage; broader scope ownership; commercial strength |
| In-house build | No margin, full control, retained knowledge | Capacity, skills gaps, opportunity cost, timeline risk, no accountability transfer | "We can do it ourselves cheaper" | Capacity and speed; build-operate-transfer; accelerators; risk transfer; coaching the in-house team |
| Status quo | Zero change risk, no procurement | Cost and risk compound; forcing events arrive anyway | (silence) | Quantified cost of delay; forcing event; low-risk first phase (assessment) |

## 2. Evidence standards for competitor claims
| Source | Confidence | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Client statement in discovery ("they missed the last upgrade date") | High | Quote in the card; never in client-facing docs |
| RFP wording mirroring the alternative's terminology or product names | Med–High | Signals authorship influence; feeds INC flags |
| Public materials (their case studies, pricing pages, job postings, press) | Med | Strengths and strategy |
| Prior encounters (our win/loss records) | Med | Patterns; check age |
| Class pattern (table above) | Low — hypothesis | Label as such |

Never: fabricate competitor weaknesses, cite rumors, or use anything obtained improperly. Never put competitor claims in client-facing material; ghost lines only.

## 3. Criteria scorecard
Rows = the client's decision criteria (sourced from RFP/discovery; otherwise `[TO BE BASELINED — owner: ___]` and the card is provisional). Columns = us, the alternative, client weight (H/M/L, sourced), and the play.

| Criterion (source) | Client weight | Us (1–5, evidence) | Them (1–5, evidence/hypothesis) | Play |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Plays:
| Play | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| Win | We score higher and client weight ≥ M | Make it a win theme; seed as an evaluation criterion |
| Neutralize | They score higher, gap closable | Partner, accelerator, named senior staff, commercial structure |
| Reframe | They score higher, gap not closable | Shift the client's attention to the consequence the criterion ignores (e.g., "continuity" → "continuity of the current run-rate") |
| Concede | They score higher, client weight L | Do not spend proposal space; acknowledge if asked |

Honesty rule: a card with no conceded criterion is not credible and will mislead the pursuit team.

## 4. Wedge logic
Wedge = criterion where (we win) ∩ (client cares, sourced) ∩ (alternative cannot neutralize quickly). One wedge is enough; two is strong; three means the criteria are too fine-grained.

If no wedge exists:
| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Client cares about criteria we lose | Reshape: propose a phase where we win (assessment, independent baseline), partner for the gap, or qualify out |
| We win on criteria the client does not care about | Educate via discovery questions and evaluation-criteria seeding; if the client will not move, qualify out |
| Alternative is entrenched and the RFP is shaped for them | Bid only if the cost of bidding is justified by relationship-building; say so |

## 5. Evaluation-criteria seeding
Neutral phrasing the client can adopt in their scoring without feeling steered:
- "Evidence of a scored inventory-based estimate with stated drivers"
- "Named delivery leadership with continuity commitments"
- "Independent baseline of current run-rate and integration incidents"
- "Parallel-run and rollback approach per migration wave"
- "Cross-platform integration references in <vertical>"
Place in: clarification questions, discovery follow-ups, the proposal's approach section, orals.

## 6. Win/loss learning
After award, record: alternative class, our wedge, client's stated reason, price delta if known, what we would change. Feed the next card. Patterns beat anecdotes; keep the register internal.
