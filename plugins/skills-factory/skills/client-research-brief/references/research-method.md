# Research Method

## 1. Emphasis by purpose
Weights are the share of the one page each section should take. Cut sections below 5% to a single line.

| Section | Discovery call | Proposal / RFP | Exec meeting (1:1) | Pitch (competitive) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Snapshot | 10% | 10% | 5% | 10% |
| Financial signals | 10% | 20% | 10% | 15% |
| Technology signals | 25% | 20% | 10% | 20% |
| Leadership | 10% | 10% | 35% | 15% |
| Recent news | 10% | 10% | 15% | 10% |
| Likely initiatives / hypotheses | 25% | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| Risks and landmines | 5% | 10% | 5% | 10% |
| Questions for the call | 5% | 5% | 5% | 5% |

## 2. Source tiers
Use the highest tier available. Cite tier in the Sources appendix so the reader can judge reliability.

| Tier | Source type | Use for | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regulatory filings (10-K/10-Q/20-F, annual report, proxy), audited accounts | Revenue, margin, segment data, stated strategy, risk factors, named programs, executive comp drivers | Public companies only; dates lag 1–3 months |
| 2 | Investor materials: earnings transcripts, investor-day decks | Initiatives with executive ownership, capex guidance, cost-out targets, timeline statements | Forward-looking; quote exact wording |
| 3 | Company press releases, regulator notices, court filings | Leadership changes, acquisitions, partnerships, enforcement | Spin; corroborate with Tier 1–2 |
| 4 | Reputable business and trade press | Context, analyst reaction, program status | Verify numbers against Tier 1 |
| 5 | Job postings, engineering blogs, conference talks, vendor case studies | Technology stack, team size, platform migrations, pain areas | Signals, not facts; rate confidence |
| 6 | LinkedIn/public profiles, social posts, review sites | Leadership tenure and background, culture signals | Public-record facts only; no inference |
| 7 | Our prior notes and relationship history (from the notes file) | Context, prior conversations, known incumbents | Internal; never appears in client-facing material |

Rules:
- A number without a Tier 1–3 source is labeled "reported" with its Tier 4–5 source, or is `[TO BE BASELINED — owner: ___]`.
- Private companies: ownership structure and public events only. No revenue estimates from aggregator sites (they are frequently wrong and cannot be defended in a meeting).
- Date every source. A 2023 job posting is a weak signal for 2026 stack.

## 3. Financial-signal extraction (public companies)
Pull exactly these from the latest annual filing and most recent quarterly; cite page or section.

| Signal | Where in the filing | Why it matters to us |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue and trend (3 years) | Selected financial data / MD&A | Size of opportunity; growth or cost-out mode |
| Operating margin trend | MD&A | Margin pressure → cost-out programs → migration/TCO plays |
| Capex and technology spend commentary | MD&A, cash-flow discussion | Funding shape (capex vs opex) |
| Named programs ("transformation", "modernization", "simplification") | MD&A, CEO letter, earnings transcript | Sponsor language to mirror in the proposal |
| Risk factors mentioning technology, cyber, legacy systems, integration, vendors | Item 1A (10-K) | Board-visible pain; quote verbatim |
| Restructuring charges | Notes to financials | Headcount reductions → capacity gaps → partner demand |
| Acquisitions / divestitures | Notes, press releases | Integration programs; platform consolidation |
| Segment reporting | Notes | Which business unit the sponsor sits in and its share |
| Auditor or control weaknesses | Item 9A | Compliance-driven urgency |

For higher-ed and public institutions: use audited financial statements, bond offering documents (very detailed on strategy and risk), accreditation reports, and board minutes where public.

## 4. Leadership profiling rules
| Include | Exclude |
|---|---|
| Title, start date, prior employer and role, public talks, quoted priorities, programs announced under them | Age, family, personal social media, speculation about internal politics or performance, anything from private sources |
| Changes since arrival (reorgs, vendor changes, hires) from Tier 3–5 sources | Characterizations ("hands-on", "political") |
| Reporting line if stated publicly | Inferred reporting line |

Format: one row per person. `Role | Name | Since | Prior | Public priorities (quoted, sourced) | Relevance to our angle`.

## 5. Likely initiatives — hypothesis format
| Initiative (hypothesis) | Signals (with tier) | Confidence | Confirming question for the call | Our angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Confidence: High = Tier 1–2 source names it; Med = two independent Tier 3–5 signals; Low = one Tier 5 signal. Do not list Low-confidence initiatives in the Bottom line.

## 6. Risks and landmines
Things that could embarrass us or kill the conversation if unknown:
- Recent outage, breach, or regulatory action (do not reference unprompted; be prepared if they do)
- Litigation with a vendor we might name
- Incumbent partners with a long relationship (check case studies and press releases)
- Executive departures in the last 90 days
- Public commitments (dates, savings targets) that any proposal must respect

## 7. Writing the brief
- Bottom line = three facts, each sourced, each with a "so what" for our angle.
- One page. The reader is walking into a meeting; they will not read page two.
- Tables for leadership, tech signals, initiatives, news.
- No adjectives about the company ("innovative", "struggling"). State the fact and the metric.
- Sources appendix: numbered, with URL or document name, section/page, date accessed, tier.
