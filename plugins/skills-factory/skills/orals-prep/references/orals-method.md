# Orals Method

## 1. Decode the evaluation
| Input | What to extract | If absent |
|---|---|---|
| RFP / invitation | Scored criteria and weights; who scores; format; what may be presented; whether pricing is discussed | `[TO BE BASELINED — owner: ___]`; ask the client contact; assume the proposal structure |
| Attendee list | Roles → what each cares about (CFO: price, risk; CIO: approach, team; procurement: compliance; operator: day-to-day) | Prepare for the four archetypes |
| Shortlist context | Number of bidders, order of presentation, time between bidders | Assume we are not last; make the close memorable |

Evaluation map: `Criterion (weight) | Slide(s) | Speaker | Proof (number, source) | Risk if challenged`.

## 2. Script structure
| Segment | Share of presenting time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | ≤90 s | Their situation (their words, one number), the outcome we commit to, what they will hear in the next <n> minutes |
| Understanding and approach | 35% | How we get to the outcome; gates; what we need from them |
| Team and governance | 20% | Who shows up, how decisions get made, continuity commitments |
| Proof | 25% | Win themes with cleared evidence; ghost lines |
| Commercial and risks | 15% | Structure, basis of estimate, top risks with mitigations (price detail only if the format allows) |
| Close | ≤60 s | The ask; next step with date; one sentence they will remember |

Rules:
- Q&A share ≥35% of total time unless the client fixes it. Evaluators remember answers more than slides.
- One speaker per segment; handoffs scripted ("<Name> will take you through how we do that").
- Every number spoken has a source in the proposal. No new numbers in the room.
- Never read slides. The slide holds the evidence; the speaker gives the "so what".

## 3. Speaker assignment
| Role on the engagement | Speaks to | Not to |
|---|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | Opening, commitments, close | Technical detail |
| Engagement lead | Approach, governance, risks, commercial structure | Deep technical |
| Lead architect | Solution, migration approach, NFRs | Pricing |
| Delivery / test lead | Factory, quality, cutover | Strategy |

Credibility rule: the person who will do the work answers questions about the work. If the proposed lead cannot attend, say so and explain who will cover; do not substitute silently.

Airtime: each presenter ≥1 segment; none >40%.

## 4. Answer discipline
| Rule | Practice |
|---|---|
| Length | ≤75 words spoken (~30 seconds). Longer answers lose the room and invite follow-ups into weak ground |
| Structure | Direct answer first → one proof (number + source) → stop. Offer detail: "Happy to go deeper if useful." |
| Unknowns | "We do not have that figure in the room; we will send it by <date>." Never guess |
| Gaps | State the gap and the mitigation. Evaluators have the compliance matrix; spin is detected |
| Competitors | Never named. Consequence language only (ghost lines) |
| Other clients | Only cleared references, in the form cleared (named or anonymized) |
| Contradictions | If an answer would contradict the proposal, the proposal wins; say "as in section <n>" |
| Pricing | Only if the format allows; otherwise "pricing is in the sealed submission; we can walk through the basis of estimate" |
| Hostile question | Acknowledge the concern; answer the underlying criterion; do not debate the premise |

## 5. Delivery rules
| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Arrive with a PDF and an offline copy | Projector/network failures |
| Names and titles of evaluators on a card | Address by name |
| One person tracks time; signals at −5 and −2 minutes | Overruns cut the close |
| Parking lot visible | Questions deferred are captured and answered in writing after |
| Silence after an answer | Do not fill it; let them ask the next question |
| Follow-up within 24 hours | Parked questions, requested figures, thank-you with the one-sentence close |

## 6. Common failure modes
| Failure | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Executive presents technical content poorly | Reassign; credibility over hierarchy |
| Script runs 40% over | Dry run 1 with timer; cut slides, not speaking pace |
| Team disagrees on an answer in the room | Hard-three rehearsal; one owner per question theme |
| New number invented under pressure | Number sheet in front of every presenter; "we will send it" rule |
| Reading the deck | Notes contain the point and the number only; no sentences |
| No close | Script the close; sponsor owns it; practice it three times |
